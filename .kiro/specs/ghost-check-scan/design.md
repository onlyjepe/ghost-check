# Design — Ghost Check scan mode

## Shape of the app

Five screens, one direction: `seal` (reference photo + slots + names) → `scan`
(camera) → `verify` (flags + corrections) → `summary` (report). `landing` picks
a mode or loads a sample kit.

State lives in one context: mode, reference photo, scan photo, slots, opacity,
toast, confirm dialog. Slots carry `{ id, label, x, y, status, confidence }`
where `x` and `y` are normalised against the photo so the same slot means the
same thing at any rendered size.

Statuses are `unchecked | present | missing | unsure`. The scan writes the last
three; the user writes `present` or `missing`. `unchecked` only exists before any
scan has run on a slot.

## How the scan decides

No model, no service. Four steps, all arithmetic on pixels:

1. **Normalise.** Both the reference photo and the captured frame are drawn into
   a 256 x 192 canvas and reduced to luma. Aspect ratio is deliberately squashed
   the same way for both inputs, which makes the comparison cheap and consistent.
2. **Align.** Both frames are blurred twice and searched over a ±8 px shift in
   2 px steps. The best shift is the one with the lowest mean absolute difference
   over the whole frame. This absorbs the small drift between two hand-held
   photos of the same case.
3. **Signature per slot.** A 16 x 16 window centred on the slot is averaged down
   into cells, then zero-meaned and unit-normalised. That makes the comparison
   invariant to exposure and white balance.
4. **Compare.** The score is the dot product of two normalised signatures, in
   `[-1, 1]`. Above 0.62 the slot reads as filled, below 0.38 as empty, anything
   between is "needs a look". Confidence is derived from the distance to the
   boundary, not presented as a probability.

Drawn demo kits score around 0.92–1.00 for filled slots and around 0.12 for the
one removed item. Real counter photos are noisier, which is exactly why the
middle band exists and why the user confirms every flag.

## Why the scan is not trusted blind

A rental counter dispute is won or lost on whether the record is right. False
"missing" flags cost the shop a customer; a missed flag costs them the item.
So the product never files on its own: the scan proposes, the person decides,
and the report screen blocks filing while any slot is still unresolved.

## Motion

Motion is used for orientation, not decoration: a sweep across the viewfinder
while a scan runs, staggered reveal of result rows, a pin pop when placed, a
bottom sheet for naming, and a page-level rise between screens. All of it is CSS
transitions and keyframes with `prefers-reduced-motion` disabling the lot.

## Colours

Ink `#0b0a0e`, panel `#1a1721`, teal `#4fe3c2` for anything the machine did,
amber `#f5b95c` for "needs a look", rust `#ff6157` for missing, orange `#ff8c4a`
for the Halloween mark on the landing screen only. The app is dark because it is
used under counter lighting against a screen, not because Halloween is dark.

## Data

Samples come from `public/demo/samples.json`, generated by `tools/make_demo.py`
from the same slot coordinates used to draw the frames. The app therefore cannot
disagree with its own artwork.

## Failure modes covered

Camera refused, no camera, camera busy, camera never ready, photo too large, file
not an image, reference replaced mid-check, scan of a wildly different frame
(low scores everywhere → everything reads "needs a look" rather than a false
clear).
